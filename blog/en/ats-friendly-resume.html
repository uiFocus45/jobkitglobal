ATS-Friendly Resume: Will Yours Get Past the Filter?

What an ATS is, what trips it up (Canva, columns, icons), a 2-minute test and the fixes that matter

Published November 28, 2026 · 3 min read

Sending out resumes and hearing nothing back, even for jobs that fit you? Before you doubt your profile, check one thing: your resume may be misread by the software that sorts applications, before a human ever sees it.

That software is called an ATS. Here is what it really does, what trips it up, and how to check your resume in two minutes.

What is an ATS?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. Many employers and recruiting agencies use one to collect, store and search applications, especially when a job ad gets a lot of them.

When you apply online, the ATS reads your resume and pulls out the text: name, contact details, jobs, dates, education, skills. Recruiters then search that database by keyword, or sort applications by what they contain.

Watch out

The real risk is not a robot that "rejects" you. It is a resume that gets misread. If the ATS mixes up your columns or misses your skills, you do not show up when the recruiter searches.

What trips up an ATS

  • Columns: a two- or three-column layout can be read line by line, mixing both sides together.
  • Text inside images: a name, heading or skills drawn as an image are not read at all.
  • Skill bars and stars: "Spanish ★★★☆☆" means nothing to software. Write "Spanish: conversational".
  • Tables and text boxes: their content is sometimes skipped or scrambled.
  • Headers and footers: some systems ignore them. Do not put your contact details only there.
  • Creative headings: "My Journey" is recognized less reliably than "Work Experience" and "Skills".
Tip

In the US, UK and Canada, leave the photo off your resume: it is not expected and it takes space from what matters.

What about Canva resumes?

Canva is not banned. The issue is heavily designed templates: columns, shapes, icons, text placed in frames. Exported to PDF, they look great to a person and confusing to software.

If you love your Canva resume, keep it for handing out in person or emailing directly to someone. For career sites and job boards, use a simple one-column version.

The 2-minute copy-paste test

  1. Open your resume PDF.
  2. Select all (Ctrl + A) and copy (Ctrl + C).
  3. Paste into Notepad or a blank email (Ctrl + V).
  4. Read the result: your name, jobs, dates and skills should appear in order and readable.

Fix your resume in 10 minutes

Do

  • One column, top to bottom
  • Standard headings: Summary, Work Experience, Education, Skills
  • The same date format everywhere (e.g. 03/2024 – 09/2025)
  • Keywords from the job ad, when they are true for you
  • A Word file (.docx) or a PDF saved from Word, named First-Last-Resume.pdf

Don't

  • Skills shown as stars or bars
  • Important text inside images or icons
  • Contact details only in the header
  • Abbreviations nobody searches for
  • The exact same resume for every job
Tip

Mirror the wording of the job ad. If the ad says "inventory management" and your resume says "stock tracking", a recruiter searching for "inventory management" may not find you. Never add a skill you do not have, though: the interview will test it.

Before you hit send

  • The copy-paste test gives clean text, in order
  • One column, no tables or text boxes
  • Contact details in the body, not only in the header
  • Standard section headings
  • Skills written in words, no stars or bars
  • 3 to 5 keywords from the job ad, when true
  • File named First-Last-Resume

A resume software can read does not have to be dull. It just has to be clear, which is also what a busy recruiter wants.
